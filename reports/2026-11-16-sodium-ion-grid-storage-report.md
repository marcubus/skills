DEEP RESEARCH REPORT
Sodium-ion grid storage in 2026
2026-11-16

BOTTOM LINE

Sodium-ion is a commercial storage technology in 2026 rather than a laboratory promise, and its shipped cells reach about 175 Wh/kg against about 205 Wh/kg for advanced lithium iron phosphate.[S1] The order book has run far ahead of the factory gate, with more than 70 GWh of supply agreements signed against a shipment target of about 1 GWh by the end of 2026.[S6][S4] The thing most readers get wrong is the price, because sodium-ion is cheaper per cell and dearer per installed system, and the two figures are quoted as though they were the same number.[S3][S8]

THE QUESTION

What sodium-ion battery storage actually delivers in 2026, measured three ways: the performance of a shipped cell, the cost of an installed system, and the volume that has left a factory rather than a press office.[S1][S4] It matters now because the technology has just crossed from demonstration into contracts, and the distance between a contract and a delivery is where most public claims go wrong.[S6] Excluded is the electric vehicle market and the home battery market, and every rival chemistry appears only where it sets the price that sodium-ion has to beat.[S1]

THE LANDSCAPE

The market has three layers, and each layer reports a different number. Cell makers sell containerised systems rather than bare cells, and CATL put its TENER Sodium unit on sale in Munich in June 2026 with more than 30 MWh of rated capacity per unit.[S4] Integrators sign the multi-year agreements that fill the order books, and the largest is a three-year 60 GWh deal between CATL and HyperStrong.[S5] Behind both sits the capacity plan, which is where the 293.7 GWh figure comes from.[S5]

The geography is lopsided. Almost all sodium-ion manufacturing sits in China, and of the four markets a November 2026 analysis examined, only India was placed in early commercial validation, backed by a 1.25 billion dollar incentive scheme and a 100 kWh pilot tender.[S6]

What changed recently is the order book rather than the factory. Supply agreements passed 70 GWh while the largest single supplier targeted about 1 GWh of cumulative shipments for the whole of 2026.[S6][S4] Anyone holding those two numbers in the same head has the story of the technology, and it is not the story the announcements tell.

FINDINGS

WHAT THE CELLS ACTUALLY DO

A commercial sodium-ion cell carries about 175 Wh/kg, against about 205 Wh/kg for advanced lithium iron phosphate.[S1] The deficit matters less on a concrete pad than under a car bonnet, because weight is not the constraint in a fixed installation.[S1] The gap has closed far enough that stationary storage is now the technology's best market rather than its fallback.[S1]

Cold is where sodium-ion wins outright. One comparison puts retained capacity in deep cold at 90 percent, and a second account describes strong low-temperature performance as a headline property of shipped cells.[S9][S1] Cycle life and safety are described as converging with lithium rather than leading it.[S1]

The competitive field is separating by duration. Sodium-ion is now a direct competitor to lithium iron phosphate for multi-hour storage, while flow, zinc, and iron-air chemistries move toward longer durations.[S1] That split is the useful frame, because it says which rival sets the price at each duration.

THE PRICE GAP IN THREE PLACES

Sodium-ion is quoted at three levels and only one of them is the price a buyer pays. At cell level the indicative range is 50 to 70 dollars per kWh, with finished packs at 70 to 100 dollars, and those are the figures that circulate as the cheap number.[S8] At system level the global average is 229 dollars per kWh against about 200 dollars for lithium iron phosphate.[S3] The difference between the two is the container, the power conversion, the controls, and the installation, none of which the cell price includes.[S8]

The lifetime case is the argument a seller makes. The same analysis projects a 21 percent lower total cost over 20 years for sodium-ion despite the higher upfront price.[S3] That figure is a projection across two decades, and it is the only basis on which the higher upfront price is defended.[S3]

ORDERS OUTRUN DELIVERIES

Sodium-ion reaches utility scale inside a lithium plant rather than beside it. Tenders are being awarded as mixed lithium-iron-phosphate and sodium configurations, so the sodium share is a minority slice of a project that would have been built without it.[S5]

The order book is real and the delivered volume is small. Eight storage tenders were awarded in the year to July 2026, totalling 170 MW and 512 MWh, while supply agreements signed worldwide passed 70 GWh.[S5][S6] The largest single commitment is the 60 GWh CATL and HyperStrong deal, and the shipment target against it is about 1 GWh by the end of 2026.[S5][S4]

THE CASE THAT DID NOT SCALE

The clearest failure is not a fire or a recall. It is the ratio between announced capacity and working capacity. Of 293.7 GWh of planned cell capacity, 25.2 GWh was operational in July 2026, about 8.6 percent, and the remainder was under construction or on paper.[S5]

The second is supply chain concentration. An analysis of four markets names an underdeveloped supply chain as India's near-term constraint while noting that China accounts for almost all sodium-ion manufacturing, which makes diversification the stated reason for buying sodium-ion and also the hardest thing to obtain.[S6]

THE NUMBERS

The system price comparison comes from a Wood Mackenzie analysis released on 5 November 2026, which puts the global average upfront cost of a sodium-ion system at 229 dollars per kWh and lithium iron phosphate at about 200 dollars per kWh.[S3] The base is the global average across the markets studied, the date is November 2026, and the method is an analyst cost model rather than a sample of invoices.[S3]

The cell price range of 50 to 70 dollars per kWh, and 70 to 100 dollars for finished packs, is published by a battery supplier in August 2026 and is described in its own text as indicative.[S8] No base is stated beyond volume and specification, and no method is given.[S8]

The capacity figure of 293.7 GWh is a count of planned cell capacity across 34 enterprises and 45 bases in 20 Chinese provinces, taken from an industry database as of July 2026.[S5] Of that, 25.2 GWh was operational, about 8.6 percent.[S5]

The order book figure of more than 70 GWh is a count of supply agreements rather than deliveries, as of November 2026.[S6] Against it, the stated target is about 1 GWh of cumulative shipments by the end of 2026, and that is a target rather than a reported figure.[S4]

The energy density comparison is 175 Wh/kg for commercial sodium-ion cells against about 205 Wh/kg for advanced lithium iron phosphate, stated in November 2026 with no test method given.[S1]

WHAT EVERYONE GETS WRONG

The common belief is that sodium-ion is the cheap battery. It is cheaper per cell and dearer per installed system, and the cell figure is the one that travels.[S8][S3]

The second belief is that a large order book means a large installed base. The gap between 70 GWh of agreements and about 1 GWh of targeted shipments is the whole of 2026 in one comparison.[S6][S4]

The third is that sodium-ion replaces lithium. In the projects actually awarded it is a minority addition inside a lithium plant, sized so that a disappointment does not sink the project.[S5]

WHERE THE SOURCES DISAGREE

The cost of sodium-ion storage differs by a factor of three between sources, from 50 to 70 dollars per kWh to 229 dollars per kWh.[S8][S3] The cause is definition rather than disagreement, since one figure is a cell or pack price and the other is an installed system, and neither source is wrong.[S8][S3]

The parity date is contested in a subtler way. Cell-level parity with lithium iron phosphate is projected by the end of 2026 among leading manufacturers, while module and system parity is projected after 2030, so the optimists and the sceptics are quoting one analysis correctly and reading different halves of it.[S3]

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS

The headline finding is that sodium-ion in 2026 is a contracted technology with a small delivered base. It would be overturned by evidence that delivered volume had reached a large fraction of the order book, which would look like a published shipment figure in the tens of gigawatt-hours for 2026 rather than a target of one.[S4] It would also weaken if system prices fell toward the cell-level range, which would show up as an installed cost near 100 dollars per kWh rather than 229.[S3]
