DEEP RESEARCH REPORT
Robot near-miss events in building 4
2026-11-16

BOTTOM LINE

The building 4 robot fleet recorded 17 near-miss events in the twelve months to February 2026, against 6 in the year before.[S1] Eleven of the 17 happened at two blind corners created by pallet racking installed in June 2025, and the committee rules out robot speed as the cause.[S1] The recommendation is to restore a second safety observer and fit convex mirrors, at an estimated 14,000 dollars.[S1]

THE QUESTION

What the building 4 safety committee record says about the rise in near-miss events, why they clustered, and what it recommends. Excluded is any comparison with other buildings, because the document covers one site only.[S1] Nothing here was verified against the outside world, since the committee record is the entire evidence.[S1]

THE LANDSCAPE

The site runs 41 autonomous mobile robots, capped at 1.2 metres per second.[S1] Pallet racking was installed in June 2025, and the committee identifies the two corners it created as the place where the events concentrate.[S1] Staffing changed in the same period, from two safety observers per shift to one.[S1]

FINDINGS

THE COUNT AND ITS DEFINITION

The fleet recorded 17 near-miss events in the twelve months to February 2026, against 6 in the prior year.[S1] The document defines a near-miss as an event where a pedestrian took evasive action or a robot performed an emergency stop within one metre of a person, so the count depends on that threshold.[S1] A different threshold would produce a different number, and the document gives no other.[S1]

THE CAUSE WAS THE RACKING

Eleven of the 17 events occurred at the two blind corners created by the racking.[S1] The committee attributes this to reduced sight lines for both the robot lidar and the pedestrian, and explicitly not to robot speed, which was capped throughout the period.[S1] The remaining six events are not located in the document.[S1]

WHAT THE CONTROLS COST

Safety observers fell from two per shift to one after a budget reduction in November 2025.[S1] The committee recommends restoring the second observer and fitting convex mirrors at the two corners, at an estimated 14,000 dollars, of which 2,000 dollars is the mirrors.[S1] The estimate carries no method.[S1]

WHAT THE DOCUMENT CANNOT SAY

The committee did not review the robot vendor's own incident log, so it cannot say whether the vendor recorded events the site did not.[S1] It also does not say how many of the 17 events involved injury, and it gives no denominator of robot hours or pedestrian movements, so the rate cannot be calculated from the document alone.[S1]
