## Status of the evidence

Three kinds of claim appear here, and they are labelled throughout.

- **Sourced**: identity, show, nationality, field, and format, from a first-party show page or the publisher. Every one carries a URL.
- **Estimate**: pace in words per minute. No audio was analysed for this file. Pace is a perceptual placement against a 150 wpm conversational anchor, and genre matters: speech rate varies systematically with task and genre, so no single figure describes all speech (Tauroza and Allison 1990). Values are given as bands, never as measurements.
- **Coded observation**: pitch register, timbre, tick habits, and emotional shifts. These are perceptual codings against the axis definitions in `references/persona-schema.md`, made from published episodes. They are not acoustic measurements and they are not quotations. No verbatim line is attributed to any host in this file.

Where a claim would need audio analysis to be more than an estimate, it is not made. Where the literature supplies a direction, the coded observation is checked against it and the citation is given.

## Emotional behaviour under strain

Four hosts, three strained states each, six measures. Everything in this section is a coded observation with ordinal magnitudes, checked for direction against L1, L2 and L5 to L7. No value here is a measurement and none should be quoted as one.

Scale: 0 no change, + slight, ++ moderate, +++ large. Rate and pause are relative to that speaker's calm baseline.

### Kim Hill

| Measure | Calm | Angry | Frustrated | Tired |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Speech rate | baseline | + (short clauses, not faster overall) | + | - - |
| Pause length | medium, used as pressure | - (interrupts instead of pausing) | - | +++ (long, unfilled) |
| Pitch ceiling | low_mixed | ++ (rises on the challenge) | + | - (drops, narrows) |
| Volume | steady | + (one step, never a shout) | + | - |
| Articulation | crisp | +++ (clipped, precise, cold) | ++ | - - (slurs, trails) |
| Interjection rate | low | - (fewer, because she cuts in) | + | + (fillers replace questions) |

Reading: her anger is cold, not hot. Rate does not climb much; articulation sharpens and pauses vanish because she stops waiting. That is the opposite of the loud-fast anger profile in L5 and L7, and it is the clearest evidence that anger has two vocal shapes.

### Louis Theroux

| Measure | Calm | Angry | Frustrated | Tired |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Speech rate | slow to measured | 0 | + | - |
| Pause length | long, deliberate | ++ (silence as pressure) | - | + |
| Pitch ceiling | mid_mixed | + (flat, controlled) | + | - |
| Volume | low | 0 or - (goes quieter) | + | - |
| Articulation | soft, slightly lazy | ++ (over-precise) | + | - - |
| Interjection rate | low | - | + (repeats the guest's words) | + |

Reading: anger reads as courtesy under pressure. The louder the guest, the quieter and more exact he becomes. Real anger shows in the pause and the precision, not the volume. This is the pattern a low-EIQ persona cannot produce, because it requires holding a state while the room moves.

### Dr Karl

| Measure | Calm | Angry | Frustrated | Intense |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Speech rate | fast | ++ | + | +++ |
| Pause length | short | - | - | - - |
| Pitch ceiling | high_mixed | ++ | + | +++ |
| Volume | high | ++ | + | ++ |
| Articulation | bright, slightly blurred | + (clipped) | + | 0 |
| Interjection rate | high | ++ (interruptions, not fillers) | ++ | + |

Reading: the hot profile. Everything rises at once, which is why it is the easiest to turn into caricature. The tell that it is real rather than performed is that articulation does not degrade until the state is extreme; shouting that also slurs reads as acting.

### Brené Brown

| Measure | Calm | Angry | Frustrated | Tired |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Speech rate | measured | + | + | - - |
| Pause length | medium | - | - | +++ |
| Pitch ceiling | low_mixed | ++ | + | - - |
| Volume | medium | ++ | + | - |
| Articulation | warm, connected | ++ (each word separated) | + | - - (breathy, trails) |
| Interjection rate | medium | - | + | + |

Reading: anger is named before it is performed. The vocal shift is real but it arrives after the sentence that labels it, which is what a high EIQ looks like from outside. Tired is the state that costs her articulation most: breath replaces support and sentences lose their ends.

## What generalises and what does not

Consistent across these four and supported by the literature:

1. Arousal raises rate, pitch ceiling and volume together for hot anger, happiness and intense curiosity (L1, L2, L7).
2. Fatigue lowers rate, pitch ceiling, pitch range, volume and articulation, and lengthens unfilled pauses (L7; the depression literature reports the same direction for flattened prosody).
3. Sadness and fatigue increase inter-word pause time (L6).
4. High-arousal states shorten pauses and increase high-frequency energy (L7).
5. Emotion is decoded more reliably from a bundle of cues than from any single cue, which is why the emotion table moves several levers at once (L1, L2).

Idiosyncratic, and therefore the part a persona must specify:

1. Direction of anger on volume. Hot anger rises; cold anger drops and sharpens. Both read as real. A profile must declare which.
2. Laugh shape and placement. Not predictable from state, and the strongest single identifier of a speaker.
3. Which interjection surfaces under load. Speakers keep their own inventory; they do not acquire a generic one.
4. Breath placement. Some speakers audibly inhale before a hard clause; others never do. This is a close-mic artefact as much as a habit.
5. Self-interruption rate. Some speakers repair constantly, others never do even when wrong.
6. Whether tiredness reads as warmth (slower, softer, more disclosure) or as impatience (shorter, flatter, clipped). Both are common; they are different personas.
