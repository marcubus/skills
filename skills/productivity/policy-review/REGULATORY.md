## The 13 Australian Privacy Principles

APP 1. Open and transparent management. A clearly expressed and up-to-date policy. Subclauses 1.7 to 1.9 add the automated decision-making disclosure.

APP 2. Anonymity and pseudonymity. Where lawful and practicable, a person can deal without identifying themselves. Relevant to services that force account creation.

APP 3. Collection of solicited personal information. Collection must be reasonably necessary, and sensitive information needs consent unless an exception applies.

APP 4. Unsolicited personal information. Destroy or de-identify it unless it could have been collected under APP 3.

APP 5. Notification of collection. At or before collection, tell the person what is collected, why, who it goes to, and the access and complaint paths. The collection notice is separate from the policy and often differs from it.

APP 6. Use and disclosure. A secondary purpose is limited to a directly related purpose where consent was not obtained.

APP 7. Direct marketing. Marketing needs consent or an opted-in expectation. The policy should state the withdrawal mechanism and whether it covers related bodies corporate.

APP 8. Cross-border disclosure. Take reasonable steps so the overseas recipient does not breach the APPs. Accountability follows the information. Section 16C provides the consent exception.

APP 9. Government related identifiers. Adoption, use or disclosure of identifiers such as Medicare or tax file numbers is restricted.

APP 10. Quality of personal information. Reasonable steps to keep it accurate, up to date, complete and not misleading.

APP 11. Security. APP 11.1 requires reasonable steps against misuse, interference, loss and unauthorised access. APP 11.2 requires destruction or de-identification when the information is no longer needed.

APP 12. Access. Respond within a reasonable period, with 30 calendar days as the OAIC guide. Refusal requires written reasons.

APP 13. Correction. Free of charge. Respond within a reasonable period, 30 days as the guide. A refused correction lets the person require a statement on the record (APP 13.4), and the entity must take reasonable steps to notify other recipients.

## Notifiable Data Breaches scheme

Part IIIC of the Privacy Act 1988. Covers eligible data breaches: unauthorised access, disclosure or loss that a reasonable person would conclude is likely to result in serious harm. Notify the OAIC and affected individuals as soon as practicable, and assess within 30 days.

## Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024

- Statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy. A disclaimer purporting to exclude liability is not a defence to the tort.
- Automated decisions transparency in APP 1.7 to 1.9.
- Children's Online Privacy Code framework. A policy that collects household composition or children's data without addressing the code is a flag now and a harder exposure later.
- Increased civil penalties and infringement notices.

## Automated decisions

Commences 10 December 2026. Applies when a computer program makes, or does something substantially and directly related to making, a decision that could reasonably be expected to significantly affect a person's rights or interests, using their personal information.

APP 1.8 requires three separate disclosures:

- The kinds of personal information used by those programs.
- The kinds of decisions made solely by those programs.
- The kinds of decisions where a program assists the decision.

Before commencement, treat an absent section as a flag under S6, not a breach. The date tells the reader whether the exposure is current or forward. A human review path is not required by the statute. Its absence is still a flag.

## APRA-regulated entities

CPS 234 applies in addition to the Privacy Act. Review points: security capability matched to threat exposure, clear ownership of information assets, incident notification to APRA within 72 hours of a material breach, and third-party arrangements including offshore.

Do not claim an entity is or is not CPS 234 compliant from its privacy policy. Say the standard applies and what the policy does not address.

## Non-Australian policies

For GDPR or UK GDPR, the same posture applies and the overlay changes: name the lawful basis, include portability and objection, and check the Chapter V transfer mechanism.

For US policies, sectoral law applies, and "we do not sell" has a statutory definition in some states that differs from the plain meaning.
