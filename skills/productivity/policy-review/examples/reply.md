# Worked reply

From a pasted policy for a note-taking service. The policy trains models on user content, shares data with partners for their own marketing, and states retention as "as long as necessary". It also names a transfer country, a breach commitment, and a minors position, so G3, G5, and G6 pass.

```text
**Bottom line**
Northwind Notes is a note-taking service. Its policy lets it train models on your notes and share data with partners for their own marketing. Nothing here is a show stopper, but each is worth acting on before you put anything sensitive in it.

**What to know**
1. **Training on your notes** (G2, S6, High attention). "We may use your content and usage information to develop and improve our services, including to train our machine learning models." (Section 4.2, How we use your information). Your notes can train their models, and the clause reads as permission already granted. Turn the training setting off, or keep client material out.
2. **Partners marketing with your data** (G1, S4, High attention). "We share information with trusted business partners who may use it for their own marketing purposes." (Section 5.1, Sharing). The word trusted sets no limit. Those partners can use your data for themselves. Opt out in the privacy settings and use a dedicated address.
3. **Retention with no maximum** (G4, High attention). "We retain information for as long as necessary for our legitimate business purposes." (Section 6, Retention). No period is stated, so the permission is unbounded. Ask for the period in writing and request deletion when you close the account.

**What is normal**
G3 transfer, G5 breach, G6 minors, and the access, correction, and complaint preferences each name a country, a period, or a route.

**What you can do**
- Turn off model training in Settings, Privacy.
- Opt out of partner sharing, and record the date.
- Keep client material in a local vault.
- Copy `POSTURE.example.md` to `POSTURE.local.md` and fill the context.

**The one question worth asking**
Which partners receive my data for their own marketing, and what contract stops them using it beyond that?

**Not legal advice.** This reads the published text on the date below.

Source: pasted text, retrieved 16 Nov 2026. Effective 1 Sep 2026. Regime: Australian Privacy Act. Posture: POSTURE.example.md.
```
