## Training and AI use

Posture: G2, S6.

Phrases: train, training data, improve our models, machine learning, develop and improve the services, fine-tune, AI features, annotate, quality assurance, "may use your content to".

Reads as training permitted when the clause covers content the reader supplies, not only aggregate metrics, and offers no opt-in.

Silent gap: no statement about AI or training anywhere.

Recommendation: where the service offers a training toggle, turn it off. Otherwise put the objection in writing to the privacy address. Keep sensitive material out of the service.

## Reuse and licensing

Posture: S3.

Phrases: perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free, sublicensable, transferable, derivative works, commercial use, adapt, modify, publish, display, distribute, "feedback you provide", residuals, "ideas you share".

A licence to host and display the reader's content is normal. The flag is width: perpetual plus sublicensable plus commercial, or a feedback clause that assigns the idea rather than a licence to use it.

Recommendation: where the reader posts original work, keep the rights-bearing copy elsewhere.

## Sharing and monetisation

Posture: G1, S4.

Phrases: trusted partners, business partners, for their own purposes, affiliates, advertising partners, data brokers, sell, rent, share, business transfer, successor entity, as permitted by law, "third parties we work with".

A transfer to a successor entity is a disclosure the reader did not choose.

Silent gap: a disclosure section that names no recipient class.

Recommendation: opt out of sharing where the service offers it, use a dedicated email address, keep account data minimal.

## Retention

Posture: G4.

Phrases: as long as necessary, indefinite, legitimate business purposes, backups, archive, legal hold, "for the life of your account".

"As long as necessary" states no maximum. It is the most common form of unbounded retention. A stated period per category is the pass condition.

Silent gap: no retention clause at all.

Recommendation: ask for the retention period in writing, request deletion on closure, keep an export before closing the account.

## Cross-border transfer

Posture: G3.

Phrases: overseas, outside your country, may be transferred, cloud providers, group companies, "countries that may change", "where our servers are located".

Silent gap: no overseas section.

Recommendation: ask which countries hold the data and what contract covers the transfer.

## Breach notification

Posture: G5.

Phrases: Notifiable Data Breaches, notify, as soon as practicable, security incident, affected individuals.

A policy that lists security measures and disclaims guarantees, with no notification commitment, fails. Naming the scheme and individual notification is the pass condition.

Silent gap: no breach section.

Recommendation: keep the account contact address current, so a notification reaches the reader.

## Children and minors

Posture: G6.

Phrases: household, number and ages of people, minors, children, parental consent, age gate, "not intended for children".

Household composition collection pulls minors in without a minors provision. That is a flag even when the service is aimed at adults.

Silent gap: no minors clause, in a service that collects household details.

Recommendation: leave household fields empty where they are optional, keep children's names out of the account.

## Rights and control

Posture: S7, S8.

Phrases: access, correct, delete, export, portability, object, withdraw consent, complaint, 30 days, fee, "contact us".

A pass names the route, the timeframe, and the complaint path. Withdrawal should be as easy as consent.

Silent gap: no access or correction section.

Recommendation: send the request in writing and keep the date, exercise the deletion right at closure.

## Risk shifting

Posture: S9, S10, S11.

Phrases: we may update, continued use, arbitration, class action waiver, liability cap, indemnify, sole discretion, not responsible, third party services, "as amended from time to time".

These live in the terms, not the privacy policy. Continued use as acceptance is the common form of unilateral change.

Recommendation: note the version and date, keep a copy of the terms accepted, raise the arbitration clause before a dispute.

## Sensitive information

Posture: S2.

Phrases: health, biometric, precise location, racial, political, sexual orientation, criminal record, government identifier, Medicare, tax file number.

Sensitive information needs explicit consent and a stated purpose. A purpose that is only implied fails.

Recommendation: withhold sensitive details the service does not need, ask what purpose is recorded.

## Surveillance

Posture: S1, S5.

Phrases: session replay, fingerprint, cross-site, monitor, scan content, access your device, cookies, pixels, "improve your experience".

Session replay and fingerprinting collect behaviour the reader did not supply. Content scanning reaches private material. Both are collection flags even when the purpose is stated.

Recommendation: use the browser's tracking controls, keep private material out of scanned surfaces, prefer the paid tier where tracking funds the free one.
