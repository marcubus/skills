The bridge failed on its first day. Not in a storm. Not under a crowd, or a truck, or a flood. In a light breeze, on a Tuesday, while the market below it was open.

And nobody was hurt?

Nobody was hurt. The deck moved, the council closed it, and the market went home.

So what did the model get wrong?

It assumed a crowd that keeps walking. The market has a base player under the eastern arch, and people stop to listen.

They stop. The model assumed they move.

The model assumed they move. Nine thousand one hundred and forty people came through on day one. The design assumed three thousand nine hundred and thirteen.

That is not a rounding error.

[sadly] No. It is a different bridge.

Who signed it?

Locky Fraser. He says he got the traffic figure after the model was fixed. The review found the figure went around three weeks before he signed.

Did he sign the revision?

He did not. He said the revision changed the assumption without changing the drawing.

And the second staircase?

Added during construction. Never in the scope. Never checked again. The review calls it the only conservative part of the structure.

Which is the part that gets me.

[warmly] Mine too. Somebody built the honest staircase by accident.


The bridge cost thirty one million. The review cost two point four. The fix is six point eight, and the market loses a season either way.

What does the review actually recommend?

Replace the bearing. Load test the span. One record, one version of each calculation. And a crowd plan for any bridge over a market.

Will they do it?

They will do the bearing. The record is the hard part.

One line. The bridge was checked against a model, and the model was wrong. Ask who checked the model before you ask who signed the drawing.
