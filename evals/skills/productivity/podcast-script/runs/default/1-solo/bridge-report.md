# Eastern footpath bridge: engineering review

Prepared for the council infrastructure committee by Priya Raman, structural engineer. 14 March.

## 1. Summary

The eastern footpath bridge opened on 2 February and closed the same afternoon. The closure followed a visible deflection of the main span under pedestrian load. Nobody was injured. The bridge remains closed pending remediation. This review covers the design, the sign-off, and the first day of use. It does not cover procurement, and it does not assign fault.

## 2. What happened

At 11:40 the market below the bridge opened. By 12:10 the footpath was carrying more people than the design assumed for a peak hour. A stallholder reported a bass note from the market PA that she said made the deck hum. At 12:26 a member of the public photographed a gap of about 40 millimetres at the eastern bearing. The council closed the bridge at 12:41. The market closed at 13:00.

The gap closed overnight. On the morning of 3 February the bearing was back within 3 millimetres of its installed position. The review says this is consistent with elastic deflection, not permanent deformation. That distinction matters: the structure moved and returned, which points to a load case the model missed rather than a failed member.

## 3. The load model

The design used a pedestrian load model that assumed 3,913 people a day, with a peak density of 0.43 people per square metre. The count on the first day was 9,140. The model also assumed that pedestrians would not stop on the span. The market makes stopping normal. People stop to look at the water, to buy, and to listen to the bass player who sets up under the eastern arch.

The model was run twice. The first run used a crowd density of 0.43. The second used 0.55. Both files are marked final. The review could not establish which run informed the drawing. The difference between them is 22% in the assumed density, which is larger than the margin in the bearing calculation.

## 4. Sign-off

The drawing was signed by Lachie Fraser on 21 December. Fraser says he was given the traffic figure after the load model was fixed. The review found the figure was circulated on 4 December, three weeks before sign-off. Fraser declined to sign the second revision. In a written statement he said the second revision changed the assumption without changing the drawing.

A second engineer, who reviewed the drawing for the contractor, says she raised the crowd question in November. Her comment is not in the project record. The review says it could not verify the comment, and does not rely on it.

## 5. The second staircase

The original scope had one staircase at the western end. The review found a second staircase in the as-built drawings, added during construction. The committee was not told. The second staircase carries about 15% of the foot traffic and is the only part of the structure the review describes as conservative.

The staircase was added to serve the market side of the bridge. The change was recorded in a site instruction, not a variation. The review says the instruction was within the contractor's delegated authority, and that the design check for the added load was not repeated.

## 6. Inspection

The bridge was inspected on 28 January, five days before opening. The inspection report records the eastern bearing as installed and aligned. It does not record a load test. The council's bridge manual requires a load test for a span over 20 metres. The eastern span is 24 metres. The review says the omission was an oversight, not a decision.

A follow-up inspection on 2 February, after closure, found no cracks in the deck and no yield in the bearings. It found the eastern bearing plate had moved 12 millimetres on its grout. The review says the movement is consistent with the deflection and does not indicate a separate fault.

## 7. Wind

The recorded gust at the time of the deflection was 11 km/h. The design wind speed is 130 km/h. Wind is not the cause. The review says the deflection is consistent with a load case the model did not include: a stationary crowd on one side of the span. A moving crowd spreads its load. A crowd that stops to listen to a bass player does not.

## 8. Records

The council's records for the project are incomplete. The review could not find the calculation for the eastern bearing. It found two versions of the load model, both marked final. It found the site instruction for the second staircase but not the design check. It found the inspection report but not the load test.

The review says the record is good enough to establish what was built and poor enough that it cannot establish why. It recommends a single project record for future work, with one version of each calculation and a named owner.

## 9. Cost

The bridge cost $31 million. The review cost $2.4 million. The remediation estimate is $6.8 million, with a range of $5.2 million to $9.1 million. The council has spent $410,000 on temporary lighting and signage. Daily foot traffic before closure was between 10,000 and 15,000 on market days.

The remediation estimate assumes the bearing is replaced and the eastern approach is strengthened. It does not assume the span is rebuilt. The review says a rebuild would cost about $19 million and would close the market for a season.

## 10. The market

The market has operated below the bridge for 31 years. Its licence does not mention the bridge. The council's events team says it was not consulted on the bridge design. The market's representative says traders were told the bridge would not change anything. The review says the consultation record is thin and recommends that future structures over the market carry a condition in the licence.

## 11. Comparable structures

The review compares the bridge with three others of similar span. All three were load tested before opening. Two carry a crowd management plan. One closes during large events. The review says the eastern footpath bridge had none of these, and that the gap is a process gap rather than a design gap.

## 12. Recommendations

1. Replace the eastern bearing and re-grout the plate.
2. Load test the span before reopening.
3. Adopt one project record with one version of each calculation.
4. Require a crowd management plan for any span over a market.
5. Repeat the design check for the second staircase.
6. Report the remediation cost to the committee each quarter.
7. Add the market condition to the licence at renewal.

## 13. What the review does not say

The review does not assign fault. It does not examine procurement. It does not cover the western staircase, which is outside the scope. It does not estimate the market's lost revenue. It does not recommend disciplinary action.

## 14. Conclusion

The bridge failed on its first day because the load model assumed a crowd that moves and a market that does not. The design was checked against the model, and the model was wrong. The record is good enough to show that, and not good enough to show who knew it. The second staircase, added without a repeated check, is the only part of the structure the review calls conservative.

The committee should treat the remediation as urgent and the record as a separate problem. The first is a bearing and a load test. The second is a habit.
